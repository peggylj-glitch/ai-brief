# The AI Brief - 2026-10-31

## Section A: Frontier AI

1. **OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic back one shared AI-training opt-out** - Announced Oct 30, the four labs will honor a single robots.txt token, __usage_ai-training, so a publisher can opt out of training across all of them at once. Anthropic says it has already started implementing it, OpenAI says in the coming months, Microsoft targets Feb 1, 2027, and Google says 2027; the signal is a voluntary IETF draft and does not remove content already in training sets. [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/google-microsoft-openai-and-anthropic-back-one-ai-training-opt-out/)

2. **OpenAI bundles enterprise privacy controls as Private Intelligence** - Announced Oct 30, the package offers Zero Data Retention for eligible API workflows and Private Inference, a confidential-computing option in preview, built with some of OpenAI's largest customers. The pitch is applying frontier models to sensitive work, but the qualifiers matter: eligibility is not spelled out and Private Inference is not yet generally available. [fourweekmba.com](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-openai-offers-zero-retention-and-private-inference-to-firms/)

3. **Anthropic's S-1 puts $16.3B in first-half revenue next to vast compute commitments** - Anthropic's Oct 26 X post announced its SEC S-1 filing for a proposed Nasdaq listing under ANT, and the filing shows $16.3B in first-half 2026 revenue, up from $1.2B a year earlier, against an $80.7B net loss driven largely by non-cash remeasurement. The filing also discloses hundreds of billions in multi-year compute commitments and a founder voting block of 50.1%, while press reports of a mid-November debut and a $100B raise remain unconfirmed by the company. [x.com](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2114705042817110293)

4. **xAI's Grok Bot can now complete purchases for you** - Announced in the run-up to Oct 30, Grok Bot can check out on a user's behalf once Link is connected, moving the agent from drafting and research to transacting. Actions run through Auto Review, with Ask-first rules producing approval cards, but xAI notes approval does not undo completed work, so spending boundaries need setting before it shops. [aitoolsrecap.com](https://aitoolsrecap.com/Blog/ai-news-october-30-2026)

5. **Canva launches Brush, a vision-language model that edits designs instead of remaking them** - Launched Oct 29 for Canva Pro subscribers, Brush reads a design's layout and layers and edits it from a single line of copy to a whole deck, without disturbing unrelated elements. Canva claims it is up to 69% faster and 46% cheaper per edit than its previous design agent, a sign that editing, not just generation, is now the product battleground. [gadgets360.com](https://www.gadgets360.com/ai/news/canva-brush-vision-language-model-launched-features-12259174)

## Section B: AI in enterprise finance functions

6. **Microsoft adds 25+ finance skills to Copilot** - Announced Oct 29, Copilot gains accounting skills and partner-built plug-ins covering close preparation, reconciliations, journal-entry drafting, audit evidence packages and forecast review, with connections to Dynamics 365, QuickBooks, Xero and Zoho Books. In seven days of limited Insider availability, more than 8,000 users invoked accounting skills over 44,000 times, suggesting finance teams will test this inside Excel first. [cfodive.com](https://www.cfodive.com/news/microsoft-expands-copilot-reach-in-finance-workflows/873239/)

7. **Intuit pilots Agentic Books Close for the monthly close** - Announced at Intuit Connect, Oct 26-28, the pilot prepares monthly close work under each firm's standards, including reconciliations, anomaly flags and recommended fixes, while logging its actions and leaving review and approval with accountants. No general-availability date has been announced, so this is a controlled test of an agent-led close, not a shipped product. [tokenpost.com](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/44033)

8. **Yooz adds agentic AI allocation for non-PO invoices** - Announced Oct 30, Agentic AI Allocation automatically applies or recommends accounting and analytical allocations for non-purchase-order invoices, extending Yooz's line-level automation beyond PO-backed invoices. High-confidence cases auto-populate, recommendations appear where review is warranted, and low-confidence decisions stay with the user, a confidence-tiered pattern worth copying in any AP rollout. [cpapracticeadvisor.com](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/10/30/yooz-adds-to-its-line-level-automation-capabilities-with-agentic-ai-allocation/192920/)

9. **AI agents are now working the order-to-cash gap at Unilever and Nestlé** - Reported Oct 30, Unilever's NOVA system handles initial order processing in about 90 seconds across 11 pilot markets, down from 15 to 20 minutes, while Nestlé uses SAP's receivables agents to prioritize collections by risk. A PYMNTS survey of 100 CFOs puts average accounts-receivable automation at 73%, up from 55% a year earlier, but 90% still cite integration complexity as the barrier to going further. [pymnts.com](https://www.pymnts.com/news/b2b-payments/2026/unilever-and-nestle-put-ai-to-work-between-orders-and-payments/)

10. **Big Tech's AI commitments now total roughly $3T off balance sheet, Morgan Stanley finds** - Reported Oct 30 from a Morgan Stanley analysis, hyperscalers and chipmakers hold about $1.1T in not-yet-started data-center leases plus $1.7T in purchase commitments, on top of about $770B in on-balance-sheet debt and leases. For FP&A leaders, the lesson is that reported quarterly capex understates committed AI spend, so lease and purchase commitments deserve a line in any 2027 capex plan. [goldenera.si](https://goldenera.si/news/big-tech-3-trillion-ai-commitments-off-balance-sheet/)

*Single-source/unconfirmed notes: Only the Anthropic S-1 item was verified from the live X post; other frontier items use web coverage. Vendor capability, benchmark, and survey claims are self-reported or company-reported, not independently verified; Anthropic's IPO timing and raise, and the $3T commitments total, are press-reported, not company-confirmed.*
